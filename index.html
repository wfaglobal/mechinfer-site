Independent evidence assessment for drug development and deployment

What does your evidence actually support?

MechInfer is an independent assessment of a programme's evidence. It takes the mechanism and the data you have, and returns the quantities you cannot measure directly, the probability that the whole path from molecule to patient holds, where that path is weakest, and the measurement that would most change the answer.

It is AI-powered, but not trained on past data: the AI reasons from a declared mechanism using Bayesian inference, so every answer can be traced and checked.

It is built on mechanism-informed inference: a declared model of the process that produced the data, fitted by Bayesian inference, reported as a calibrated distribution rather than a point estimate. Where the data cannot support a quantity at all, that is the finding, and it is reported as such rather than computed anyway.

The page below is arranged so that it can be checked: the benchmark, the reanalyses, the document you would receive, the method, and what the method does not do.

Benchmark

The framework is checked against published reference values rather than against itself. On the standard population pharmacokinetic benchmark — the theophylline dataset, 12 subjects, 132 samples — it recovers the published estimates:

The claim that matters is not the agreement in the last column but the calibration: the 95% posterior intervals contain 94.7% of the observations, against a nominal 95%. An interval that is honest about its own width is the only kind worth propagating into a decision.

02

Reanalyses

Published studies, reanalysed with the same core, to show what the method finds in real data. These are methodological: the point is what a design can and cannot support, not a verdict on anyone's science.

The plateau was reached before the first sample

In a published gut–liver barrier transport study, the signal had levelled off before the first measurement at 30 minutes. A permeability calculated from those points describes the sampling schedule rather than the barrier, so it is reported as not estimable. One sample inside the first ten minutes would recover it. The same fit confirms the authors' conclusion on the combination of agents and puts an interval on it: 1.90× control, 1.61 – 2.22.

Reanalysis of published transport data, two-concentration control check: observed 11.6×, interval 5.3 – 36.1, containing the predicted 20×.
A 2.5× median ambiguity, from one assumption

In a published single-concentration drug screen, the Hill slope cannot be identified from one concentration per compound. Fixing it is unavoidable; what it costs is measurable. Across plausible slopes the implied potencies move by a median factor of 2.5, and for the most potent compounds by 33. Three of eighteen measurements report no activity — those are bounds, not values, and are reported as bounds.

04

Method

  • A mechanism, declared in advance

    The process that produced the data is written down, with parameters that mean something physically: clearance, permeability, potency. Nothing is learned from a corpus. The structure is declared, so it can be argued with, and the fit can contradict it.

  • Uncertainty carried along the chain

    A development decision rests on a sequence of separate studies. Each is propagated on common posterior draws, so correlations induced upstream survive into the joint result. The chain reports two different weakest links — the one least likely to hold, and the one least well evidenced. They are rarely the same link, and reporting only the first is how a chain of weak evidence comes to look like a confident conclusion.

  • The next experiment, valued against the decision

    Every uncertain quantity is ranked by how much measuring it would change what the programme does, at a stated measurement precision rather than a perfect one. A quantity can carry wide uncertainty and still be worth nothing to measure, if no value it could take would alter the decision. Weak evidence and decision relevance are different things, and only one of them is worth an experiment.

05

What it does not do

  • It does not predict clinical outcome. It states what the supplied evidence supports under stated assumptions, and names the assumptions.
  • It cannot supply information an experiment did not contain. It can avoid discarding what is there, and refuse to invent what is not.
  • A mechanism may be incomplete or wrong. Conclusions are conditional on it, and must be tested under the conditions the decision will be made in.
  • It is not a regulatory qualification. Where a method is intended for a regulatory submission, the assessment documents the evidence for that specific context of use; the qualification itself is the agency's to grant.
  • Every assessment is signed by a named person who is accountable for it. Inference can propagate uncertainty and rank experiments; it cannot be accountable.
06

What an engagement looks like

What you send
The data as you have it, the mechanism as you understand it, and the decision you are facing — stated as a threshold, since the value of any measurement depends on what you would do differently. Published or summary-level data is enough to begin.
What comes back
A signed assessment: the quantities inferred with their intervals, the probability the path holds, both weakest links, the experiments ranked by what they are worth against your threshold, the assumptions register, and the limits. Where a quantity is not estimable from the design, that finding and the design change that would fix it.
How long
Two to four weeks for a single programme, depending on how much of the mechanism has to be specified from scratch.
First conversation
A reanalysis of something already published in your area, at no cost, so the method is judged on your own subject matter rather than on this page.
